1. Our Commitment to Fair and Legal Use

Streamy Music is built on a foundation of respect for the digital ecosystem. We use a variety of third-party application programming interfaces (APIs), public data services, and open-source tools to bring you a comprehensive music streaming experience. We believe that technology thrives when everyone plays by the rules. That is why we make every reasonable effort to follow the terms of service, rate limits, and usage policies set by every single provider we work with.

We do not attempt to circumvent restrictions, we do not hide our usage patterns, and we do not repurpose data in ways that conflict with the original intent of the provider. Our team reviews provider guidelines regularly and adjusts our integration architecture whenever policies change. If you have any questions about how we interact with a specific provider, please reach out to us at support.streamymusic@gmail.com.

2. Limitations and Responsible Consumption

Wherever possible, we limit our API usage to stay comfortably within the free tiers and fair-use boundaries defined by our partners. When a provider offers a public API with documented quotas, we cache responses intelligently, batch requests efficiently, and avoid unnecessary polling. Our backend infrastructure is designed to minimize redundant calls and to respect rate-limit headers returned by every endpoint. We review our traffic patterns monthly and adjust caching strategies whenever a provider changes their rate-limit policy or quota structure.

We also implement fallback mechanisms so that temporary service interruptions from one provider do not degrade your overall experience. If one lyrics service becomes temporarily unavailable, the app gracefully degrades rather than failing. If a metadata provider experiences downtime, we surface cached information from prior requests to keep browsing functional. This balanced approach protects both our users and our partners, ensuring a sustainable relationship with every data source we rely on.

Our caching layer operates on multiple levels. At the application level, we store frequently accessed metadata in memory with carefully tuned TTL (time-to-live) values that respect provider refresh cycles. At the infrastructure level, we use distributed caching to reduce repeated geographic lookups and artist biography requests. At the client level, we preload only the data strictly necessary for the current view, avoiding speculative fetches that would waste provider resources and user bandwidth alike.

Rate limiting is not treated as an obstacle to work around; it is treated as a contract we willingly accept. Our request schedulers honor the Retry-After headers returned by providers, back off exponentially when limits are approached, and alert our engineering team when sustained load approaches any threshold. We view our API providers as valued collaborators, not infinite utilities, and we engineer accordingly.

Furthermore, we monitor error rates and response times for every integrated endpoint through automated health checks. Anomalous patterns trigger immediate investigation, and if a particular integration is found to be abnormally demanding, we proactively reduce its request volume and contact the provider to discuss optimization. This diligence is not merely defensive; it reflects our conviction that long-term partnerships are built on mutual respect and operational discipline.

3. YouTube – Official API Integration

YouTube Data API v3 & IFrame Player API

Streamy Music integrates with YouTube exclusively through the official YouTube Data API and the YouTube IFrame Player API, both provided by Google LLC under their published terms of service. We do not scrape, record, or download any video or audio files from YouTube. All playback happens through the official embedded player, which remains visible during playback in accordance with YouTube's platform requirements.

We do not support background playback with the screen turned off, we do not cache full media files locally, and we do not permit users to extract audio tracks from YouTube videos. Our usage is limited to searching, browsing, and streaming content exactly as YouTube's APIs are designed to allow. We are grateful for the extraordinary catalog of music and video content that YouTube and its creators make available, and we are committed to preserving that relationship through fully compliant use.

4. Apple iTunes – Public Search API

Apple iTunes Search API

We use the publicly available Apple iTunes Search API to retrieve metadata such as song titles, artist names, album artwork, release dates, and genre classifications. This API is offered by Apple Inc. as a free, public resource for developers. We access it within the documented request limits and cache responses responsibly to reduce repeated queries.

All artwork, metadata, and related content displayed through the iTunes API remain the intellectual property of Apple, the artists, and the record labels. We do not claim ownership of any material retrieved through this service. Our usage is limited strictly to enriching the browsing and discovery experience within Streamy Music.

5. LRCLIB – Open Lyrics Database

LRCLIB is a community-driven, open-source repository of synchronized song lyrics provided free of charge to developers and music enthusiasts. Streamy Music queries LRCLIB to display lyrics alongside the tracks you play. We respect the open nature of this resource by attributing data where appropriate and by not overwhelming the service with excessive request volume.

Lyrics content belongs to the respective songwriters, publishers, and rights holders. LRCLIB serves as an aggregation layer, and we display this content in good faith for personal, non-commercial use only.

6. AUDD – Audio Recognition Service

AUDD provides audio fingerprinting and recognition technology that allows users to identify songs by recording a short audio snippet. Streamy Music integrates with AUDD's API to power our sound-recognition feature. We send only the minimum audio fingerprint required for identification, and we do not store, share, or retain any recorded audio beyond the instant needed to complete the recognition query.

All audio recognition requests are handled in accordance with AUDD's terms of service and privacy policy. We are committed to user privacy and to transparent handling of any audio data involved in the recognition process.
